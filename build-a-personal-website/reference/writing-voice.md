# Writing voice

The design is quiet on purpose so the *writing* carries the personality. Help the person sound like themselves — not like marketing. This is the difference between a site that reads as real and one that reads as a template.

## Principles
- **Plain and honest.** Say the true thing plainly. No hype, no superlatives, no "passionate about leveraging synergies."
- **Not persuasion.** The site documents what someone is doing and thinking; it isn't a sales pitch. Don't try to convince the reader of anything. Let the work speak.
- **Specific over clever.** Concrete details ("a trading bot built with Claude Code") beat vague claims ("innovative solutions").
- **Their words.** If the person has written anything — old posts, a bio, notes — mirror their phrasing and rhythm. Match how they actually talk.
- **First person, active voice.** "I build with AI to understand how it works," not "AI solutions are built."

## Homepage / bio copy
Keep it short and unforced. A one-line tagline and, if anything, a plain sentence or two. Resist the urge to add a "hooky" headline or a "Currently…" section that reads as selling. If it feels like a pitch, cut it.

## Blog posts
Each post: a clear `title`, a `tldr` (one honest sentence or two summarizing the piece — built from the post's own points, not a teaser), and a Markdown `body`.
- Openings state the subject directly. No throat-clearing.
- Short posts are fine. A few paragraphs and a list can be a complete post.
- Show the thinking, including what didn't work. "Some of these projects work. Others showed me how wide the gap is between a demo and a dependable system" reads as real; "I built amazing AI systems" does not.
- End when the point is made.

## Projects copy
- Title = what it is, plainly.
- Description = one or two sentences on what it does and who it's for. No adjectives doing work that facts should do.
- If there's nothing to show yet, "Coming soon" with a real title (a roadmap) is honest and looks intentional. Don't invent fake accomplishments.

## Quick test
Read it back and ask: *would a real person say this out loud to a colleague?* If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it plainer. If it's trying to convince, cut the convincing and just state what's true.
